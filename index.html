The six leaks

Every shop I open has at least three of these

Tap any one to see how to check it yourself today. You don't need me for this part.

01The phone nobody answers

Your ad gets the call. The front desk is under a car. The call drops. In Google Ads that call looks identical to a successful one — the money is spent either way, and the caller is already dialling the next shop in the results.

Check it: open your call log, count unanswered calls during business hours last week, multiply by your cost per click. That number is real.

02Two checkboxes Google ticks for you

Search Partners and the Display Network are on by default in every new search campaign. They spend your budget on other people's websites and on mis-taps inside mobile games. Cheap clicks, great CTR, no cars in the bay.

Check it: Campaign → Settings → Networks. If both are ticked, you just found where most of the budget goes.

03Everything pointed at the homepage

Someone searched "clutch replacement near me" and landed on a page that says you've been in business since 2009 and lists twenty services. They will not hunt for theirs. They leave, and the click is already paid for.

Check it: click your own ad from your phone. Is the thing you searched for the first thing on screen, with a price and a phone number?

04A site that loads in six seconds

On a phone, on mobile data, in a parking lot. A third of people are gone by second three. You paid full price for every one of them and they never saw your shop.

Check it: run your site through PageSpeed Insights, Mobile tab. Look at LCP. Over 2.5 seconds means you lose people daily.

05The lead that waited six hours

A form goes to an inbox nobody opens in the shop. The customer filled out three forms in ten minutes and drove to whoever replied first. Your lead count looks fine. Your bays don't.

Check it: submit your own form from your personal phone and time the reply. Most shops are shocked.

06Ads that ran out of money by noon

Or stopped nine days ago because a card expired and nobody got an email they read. Slow week gets blamed on the season. The account was simply off.

Check it: open your account and look at yesterday's spend. Zero means something broke, not that the market died.
